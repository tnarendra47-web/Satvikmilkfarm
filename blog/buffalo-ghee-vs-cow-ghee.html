Food technologists also use lab numbers such as the RM value and Kirschner value, which are typically higher in buffalo ghee. These need a laboratory; they are not home tests.

Taste, aroma and grain are personal preferences, and both are used in everyday Gujarati and North Indian cooking, from dal tadka to sweets. If you have a specific dietary need, ask your doctor or dietitian.

Can you check at home which ghee it is?

Only roughly. Colour is a helpful hint, but it varies with season and feed. A 2022 peer-reviewed study found that cow ghee mixed with up to 20% buffalo ghee still fell inside the normal range of pure cow ghee for most standard tests (BR reading, RM and Polenske values, and most colour values); only the Kirschner value and whiteness index separated them. So the most practical check is to buy from a seller you can ask questions, and to look at the label or ask for details.

What is the main difference between cow ghee and buffalo ghee?

The easiest difference to see is colour. FSSAI's guidance note describes good ghee as bright yellow for cow milk and white with a greenish tinge for buffalo milk. Research links the yellow colour of cow ghee to fat-soluble carotenoids, which buffalo ghee largely lacks. Taste and aroma are a matter of personal preference.

Can I tell pure cow ghee from buffalo ghee at home?

Not reliably. Colour is a useful hint, but a 2022 study found that cow ghee mixed with up to 20% buffalo ghee still fell within the normal range of pure cow ghee for most standard lab tests; only some tests (Kirschner value and whiteness index) separated them.
