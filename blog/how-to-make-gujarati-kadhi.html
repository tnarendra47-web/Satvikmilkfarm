How to Make Gujarati Kadhi at Home with Curd or Chaas (Without Curdling)

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 25 November 2026

Quick answer: Whisk about 1½ cups of sour, room-temperature curd with 2 tablespoons of besan until there are no lumps, then whisk in about 3 cups of water, ginger, green chilli, a little sugar or jaggery and salt. Heat on medium, stirring all the time until it boils, then simmer on low. Add a ghee tadka (mustard seeds, cumin, cloves, cinnamon, dry red chilli, curry leaves, hing), simmer about 5 minutes and finish with coriander. You can use chaas instead of curd and water. Kadhi (કઢી, कढ़ी) is the thin, warm curd-and-besan curry that sits next to khichdi or rice in almost every Gujarati home. People search for it as gujarati kadhi recipe, kadhi kaise banaye ki fate nahi, how to make kadhi from buttermilk, ગુજરાતી કઢી બનાવવાની રીત or कढ़ी कैसे बनाएं. This guide sums up what published Gujarati kadhi recipes agree on, and where they differ.

What is Gujarati kadhi?

Gujarati kadhi is a sweet-and-sour, thin kadhi made from sour dahi or buttermilk thickened lightly with besan (gram flour) and finished with a ghee tadka. It is different from Punjabi kadhi, which is thicker and usually served with onion pakoras. Turmeric is optional: many Gujarati households leave it out, so the kadhi stays white or pale.

How to make Gujarati kadhi, step by step

Step 1: Use sour curd at room temperature

Take the curd out of the fridge a few hours ahead. Recipe writers say to leave it out about 3–4 hours in hot weather and 6–8 hours in winter if you want it more sour. Cold curd is the most common reason kadhi splits.

Step 2: Whisk curd and besan until completely smooth

In the pan you will cook in, whisk the curd with the besan, ginger, green chilli and sugar until there are no lumps at all. A hand blender helps. Lumps cannot be fixed once the pan is on the stove.

Step 3: Add water

Whisk in about 3 cups of water to make a thin, smooth mixture. It will look watery at this stage; it thickens as it cooks.

Step 4: Heat on medium and stir until it boils

Put the pan on medium heat and stir constantly until it comes to a boil, usually a few minutes. Do not use high heat. Once it boils, turn the heat down to low so it does not boil over.

Step 5: Make the tadka

Heat ghee in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and cumin and let them splutter, then the dry red chilli, cinnamon and cloves, and finally the curry leaves and hing. Pour the tadka into the hot kadhi and stir.

Step 6: Simmer and finish

Simmer on low for about 5–10 minutes so the raw taste of besan goes away, stirring now and then. Taste for salt and sweetness, add chopped coriander and switch off the stove.

How to make kadhi with chaas (buttermilk)

Gujarati kadhi has long been made with buttermilk as well as dahi. Chaas is already curd thinned with water, so use the chaas in place of the curd-and-water mixture and whisk the besan straight into it. Because chaas is more watery than curd, it needs a little more besan for the same body, just as non-fat curd does. Use plain chaas; masala chaas already has salt and spices, so taste before adding more. See our chaas vs masala chaas guide.

Why does kadhi curdle? (kadhi fate nahi, iske liye)

  • Cold curd: curd straight from the fridge gets a temperature shock and splits. Use it at room temperature.
  • High heat: cook on medium, then low. Do not boil it hard.
  • Not stirring: stir constantly from the moment the heat is on until it boils.
  • Too little besan or lumps: besan holds the kadhi together. Watery curd needs more, and it must be whisked in fully before heating.
  • Pouring cold curd into hot tadka: if you do the tadka first, add the curd mixture slowly while stirring. Adding the tadka at the end avoids this.
  • Salt and lemon: recipes disagree on salt timing; if your kadhi splits often, add salt after it has boiled. Add lemon juice, if your curd is not sour, only after switching off the stove.

Full-fat or non-fat curd for kadhi?

Both work. Full-fat curd gives a creamier kadhi with less besan; non-fat curd is lighter but needs about 3½–4 tablespoons of besan for 1½ cups. Sour curd matters more than fat level. Read our full-fat vs non-fat curd guide or make your own with our guide to curd at home.

What to serve with Gujarati kadhi

The classic pairing is kadhi-khichdi. It is also served with plain steamed rice (kadhi-chawal), roti or thepla, and some people drink it like a soup.

How to store kadhi

  • Let it cool, then keep it in an airtight container in the fridge at 4 °C or below.
  • Recipe sources suggest using it within about 2–5 days. It thickens as it cools; reheat gently on low heat with a splash of water.
  • Recipe writers advise not freezing kadhi, because the curd separates when thawed.
  • Do not leave cooked kadhi out for more than two hours (one hour in very hot weather), as the US FDA advises for perishable food.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make Gujarati kadhi at home?

Whisk about 1½ cups of sour curd with 2 tablespoons of besan until smooth, then whisk in about 3 cups of water, ginger, green chilli, a little sugar or jaggery and salt. Heat it on medium, stirring all the time until it comes to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer. Pour over a tadka of ghee with mustard seeds, cumin, cloves, cinnamon, dry red chilli, curry leaves and hing, simmer about 5 minutes more and add coriander.

Why does my kadhi curdle (kadhi fat jati hai)?

Recipe writers give the same main reasons: curd straight from the fridge (temperature shock), cooking on high heat, not stirring until it boils, too little besan for watery curd, and lumps of besan. Use room-temperature curd, whisk it completely smooth with the besan before heating, keep the flame medium and stir constantly until it starts to boil.

How much besan do I need for kadhi?

One widely used Gujarati kadhi recipe uses 2 tablespoons of besan for 1½ cups of full-fat curd, 3 tablespoons for low-fat curd and 3½ to 4 tablespoons for non-fat curd. The more watery the curd, the more besan you need. Gujarati kadhi is meant to be thinner than Punjabi kadhi, so do not make it too thick.

Can I make kadhi with chaas (buttermilk) instead of curd?

Yes. Gujarati kadhi is traditionally made with dahi or buttermilk. Chaas is already curd thinned with water, so use the chaas in place of the curd-and-water mixture and whisk the besan into it. Thin chaas needs a little more besan for body. Use plain chaas, not masala chaas, unless you want the extra spices.

Why is Gujarati kadhi sweet?

Gujarati kadhi is a sweet-and-sour kadhi. Recipes add sugar or jaggery (about 2–3 teaspoons for 4 servings) to balance the sour curd. It is also thinner and paler than Punjabi kadhi, which is thicker and usually served with pakoras. Turmeric is optional; many Gujarati homes leave it out, so the kadhi stays white.

Should I add salt at the start or the end?

Recipes disagree. Some add salt before heating, others advise adding it only after the kadhi has boiled. If your kadhi often splits, add the salt after it comes to a boil. If your curd is not sour enough, add a few drops of lemon juice only after switching off the stove.

How long does kadhi last in the fridge?

Let it cool, then keep it in an airtight container in the fridge. Recipe sources suggest using it within about 2–5 days. It thickens as it cools, so reheat it gently on low heat with a splash of water. Recipe writers advise against freezing because the curd separates. Do not leave cooked kadhi out for more than two hours.
