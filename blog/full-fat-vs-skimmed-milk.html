Full-Fat vs Skimmed Milk: Which Should You Order?

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Prices checked 6 November 2026

Quick answer: The main difference is fat and calories. Full-fat (often called full cream) milk is creamier and higher in energy. Skimmed milk has almost all fat removed (FSSAI: not more than 0.5% fat) and fewer calories per glass, while protein and calcium stay broadly similar. What "full-fat" and "skimmed" mean under FSSAI

Indian food law sets minimum fat and solids for each class of milk. Under the Food Safety and Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations, 2011 (Chapter 2.1 Dairy products and analogues), the all-India figures commonly cited are:

Class of milkMilk fatMinimum SNF (solids-not-fat)
Full cream milknot less than 6.0%9.0%
Toned milknot less than 3.0%8.5%
Double toned milknot less than 1.5%9.0%
Skimmed milknot more than 0.5%8.7%
Cow milk (species standard)not less than 3.2%8.3%

Source: FSSAI Compendium, Chapter 2.1 Dairy products and analogues. These are legal standards at the point of sale, not the lab result for any one farm bottle.

Nutrition: fat and calories vs protein and calcium

Removing fat mainly lowers energy; protein and calcium stay in a similar range. ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition's Indian Food Composition Tables 2017 list average values for whole cow milk around ~73 kcal, ~4.5 g fat, ~3.3 g protein and ~118 mg calcium per 100 g. Skimmed cow milk in the same tables is much lower in fat and energy, with protein and calcium still in a comparable band (exact table entries vary by sample).

FocusFull-fat / whole styleSkimmed style
Fat & caloriesHigher; creamier mouthfeelMuch lower fat (≤0.5% under FSSAI); fewer kcal per glass
Protein & calciumPresent in the non-fat solidsAlso present in the non-fat solids — similar order of magnitude
Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)Travel with the creamLower unless the milk is separately fortified (check the label on packet brands)
TasteRicher, thickerLighter; some people find it thinner

Composition source for whole cow milk averages: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017 (IFCT 2017), ICMR–NIN, Hyderabad. Values are regional averages; actual milk varies with breed, feed and season. This is general information, not a health claim for our products.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose full-fat milk if you want a creamier glass for drinking, chai, or cooking where richness matters.
  • Choose skimmed milk if you want lower fat and fewer calories per litre while still using milk for protein and calcium.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, full-fat milk or skimmed milk?

Neither is better for everyone. Full-fat (full cream) milk has more milk fat and calories, so it tastes creamier. Skimmed milk has almost all fat removed (FSSAI: not more than 0.5% fat) and fewer calories per glass, while protein and calcium stay similar. Choose by taste, calorie needs and what your doctor advises.

What is the difference between full cream and skimmed milk?

The main difference is fat. Under FSSAI standards, full cream milk must have at least 6.0% milk fat and 9.0% solids-not-fat; skimmed milk must have not more than 0.5% fat and at least 8.7% solids-not-fat. Protein and calcium are largely in the non-fat portion, so they stay similar.

Does skimmed milk have less protein or calcium?

Generally no. Protein and calcium are mostly in the solids-not-fat part of milk. Removing fat mainly cuts calories and fat-soluble vitamins that travel with the cream. ICMR-NIN IFCT 2017 whole cow milk averages about 3.3 g protein and 118 mg calcium per 100 g; skimmed cow milk is in a similar range for protein and calcium with much lower fat and energy.

Is full-fat or skimmed milk better for kids?

It depends on the child's age and needs. Please ask your paediatrician before choosing or switching milk, especially for babies and toddlers. We do not give medical advice.
