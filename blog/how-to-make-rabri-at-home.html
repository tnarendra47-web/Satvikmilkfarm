How to Make Rabri (Rabdi) at Home with Milk: Step by Step

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 21 November 2026

Quick answer: Pour about 1–1.25 litres of full-fat or buffalo milk into a wide, heavy kadai and keep it at a gentle simmer, never a rolling boil. Each time a skin of malai forms, push it to the side of the pan. When the milk has reduced to about half, stir in sugar to taste, saffron and cardamom. Keep going until about one-third is left (roughly 1 to 1¼ hours for 1.25 litres), scrape the malai back in, and serve warm or chilled. One litre gives roughly 300–350 ml. Rabri (રબડી, रबड़ी), also written rabdi, is a sweet, thickened milk dessert full of soft flakes of malai. It is eaten on its own, chilled, or with jalebi, malpua and gulab jamun. People search for it as how to make rabri at home with milk, rabdi kaise banaye ghar par, रबड़ी कैसे बनाएं घर पर or રબડી બનાવવાની રીત. This guide sums up what published home recipes and Indian dairy-technology material agree on, and shows which milk from our shop to use.

Note: this is a general home recipe. Rabri is not listed on our shop, and this article does not describe any Satvik process.

What is rabri?

Dairy-technology course material describes rabri as a sweetened, concentrated whole-milk product with a thickened malai layer, made by slowly evaporating milk. The milk is kept just below the boil so that clotted cream forms on the surface. That cream is lifted off again and again and kept on the cooler sides of the pan, then mixed back in at the end. The more malai it has, the better the rabri is rated.

Rabri vs basundi

Both start from sweetened, high-fat milk reduced on the stove. A NIFTEM write-up under the PMFME scheme explains the difference: in rabri the malai is collected and kept as flakes, while basundi is cooled without letting a thick cream layer form, and any cream is mixed in evenly so the texture is smooth and uniform.

Why buffalo milk is preferred

Both the dairy course material and the NIFTEM write-up say buffalo milk is preferred, because its higher fat forms a thicker malai layer. Full-fat cow milk works too. Recipes advise against low-fat or skimmed milk, and note that homogenised milk gives thinner malai layers.

Use a heavy, thick-bottomed pan with a wide surface, such as a kadai. A wide pan evaporates the milk faster and lets you collect more malai.

How to make rabri at home, step by step

Step 1: Heat the milk slowly

Pour the milk into the kadai and heat it on low to medium heat, stirring a few times. It should simmer gently with small bubbles, not boil, so that a skin of cream can form on top.

Step 2: Collect the malai on the sides

When a layer of malai forms, use a spatula to push it gently to the side of the pan, where it sticks. Repeat every time a new layer forms. Every few minutes, scrape the bottom of the pan so the milk does not catch and burn.

Step 3: Add sugar and flavour at about half

When the milk has reduced to about half, add the sugar, crushed saffron and cardamom and stir gently. Keep simmering, scraping and collecting malai. Once the milk has lost about half its volume it thickens quickly and is more likely to scorch, so lower the heat and stir more often.

Step 4: Reduce to about one-third

Stop when about one-third of the milk is left. For 1.25 litres this takes about 1 hour to 1 hour 15 minutes, depending on your pan. Scrape the malai from the sides back into the thick milk, breaking it into small flakes.

Step 5: Serve warm or chilled

Rabri thickens further as it cools, so keep it slightly thin if you plan to chill it. Let it cool, then refrigerate in a covered steel container for a few hours. Garnish with sliced almonds and pistachios.

How much rabri will 1 litre of milk give?

Home recipes reduce milk to about one-third, which gives roughly 300–350 ml of rabri per litre (one recipe makes about 300–400 ml from 1.25 litres). The NIFTEM write-up says a yield of about 25–28% can be expected from buffalo milk. The exact amount depends on the milk's solids, how far you reduce it and how much sugar you add.

For example, 2 litres of Buffalo Milk cost ₹160 at today's shop price and give very roughly 600–700 ml of rabri.

How to store homemade rabri

  • Keep it in an airtight container in the fridge at 4 °C or below. One recipe guide says homemade rabri keeps for 3–4 days this way.
  • Longer shelf lives quoted for packaged rabri (for example about 10 days at 4 °C) are for factory-made product and do not apply to homemade rabri.
  • Do not leave it out for more than two hours (one hour in very hot weather), as the US FDA advises for perishable food.
  • Use a clean, dry spoon, and throw it away if it smells sour or shows mould.

Common problems and simple fixes

The milk boiled over or there is little malai

The heat was too high. A rolling boil stops the cream from forming a skin. Keep it at a gentle simmer, and use buffalo or full-fat milk rather than homogenised or low-fat milk.

The rabri tastes burnt

The milk caught on the bottom. Use a heavy pan, scrape the bottom every few minutes, and lower the heat once the milk has reduced by half. Do not scrape burnt bits into the rabri.

The rabri is too thin or too thick

Too thin: simmer it a little longer. Too thick after chilling: stir in a few spoons of warm milk.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make rabri at home with milk?

Heat full-fat or buffalo milk in a wide, heavy kadai on low to medium heat so it simmers but does not boil. Each time a layer of cream (malai) forms on top, push it to the side of the pan. When the milk has reduced to about half, add sugar, saffron and cardamom, keep simmering until about one-third of the milk is left, then scrape the collected malai back in and serve warm or chilled.

Which milk is best for rabri?

Dairy-technology course material says buffalo milk is preferred for rabri, because its higher fat forms a thicker layer of malai. Full-fat cow milk also works. Recipes advise against low-fat or skimmed milk, and say homogenised milk gives thinner malai layers.

How much rabri does 1 litre of milk make?

Home recipes reduce the milk to about one-third, which gives roughly 300–350 ml of rabri per litre, or about 300–400 ml from 1.25 litres. A NIFTEM (PMFME scheme) write-up says a yield of about 25–28% can be expected from buffalo milk, depending on the milk's solids, how far it is reduced and how much sugar is added.

How long does it take to make rabri?

One published recipe takes about 1 hour to 1 hour 15 minutes for 1.25 litres of milk, depending on the size and type of pan. A wider pan evaporates faster. Traditional sweet-shop rabri is simmered for several hours.

How much sugar goes into rabri?

Dairy-technology course material gives sugar at about 5–6% of the milk, which is roughly 50–60 g per litre. Home recipes add it to taste, for example 2.5–3 tablespoons for 1.25 litres, once the milk has reduced to about half.

What is the difference between rabri and basundi?

Both are made by reducing sweetened, high-fat milk. In rabri the layers of malai are collected and kept as flakes in the thick milk. Basundi is cooled without letting a thick cream layer form, and any cream is mixed in evenly so it has a smooth, uniform texture.

How long does homemade rabri last?

One recipe guide says homemade rabri keeps 3–4 days in an airtight container in the fridge. Keep it at 4 °C or below, do not leave it out for more than two hours, and throw it away if it smells sour or shows mould. Longer shelf lives quoted for packaged rabri apply to factory-made product.
