Malai paneer vs low fat paneer: what FSSAI says

Indian food law sets paneer types by moisture and by milk fat on a dry-matter basis. Under the Food Safety and Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations, 2011, sub-regulation 2.1.16 (Chhana and Paneer), as amended:

TypeMoisture (max)Milk fat, dry-matter basis
Paneer (full fat)60.0%not less than 50.0%
Medium fat paneer60.0%more than 20.0% and less than 50.0%
Low fat paneer70.0%not more than 20.0%

Source: FSSAI Compendium, Chapter 2.1 Dairy products and analogues, 2.1.16. These are legal standards, not the lab result for any one batch.

"Malai paneer" is the everyday trade name for the richer, full-fat style. We list Malai Paneer and Low Fat Paneer as product names on our shop. We do not publish a lab fat % for each batch on the website; message us on WhatsApp if you need more detail.

What is the difference between malai paneer and low fat paneer?

The main difference is fat. Under FSSAI standards, regular (full-fat) paneer must have at least 50% milk fat on a dry-matter basis and not more than 60% moisture; low fat paneer has not more than 20% milk fat on a dry-matter basis and up to 70% moisture. 'Malai paneer' is the common trade name for the richer, full-fat style.

Which paneer is better, malai or low fat?

It depends on the dish and your needs. Malai paneer is softer and richer, so many people prefer it for gravies like paneer butter masala or palak paneer. Low fat paneer has less fat and is firmer, and suits tikka, bhurji or salads. For personal diet advice, please ask your doctor or dietitian.
