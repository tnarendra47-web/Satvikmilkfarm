How to Make Shrikhand at Home from Curd (Dahi): Step by Step

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 20 November 2026

Quick answer: Tie about 1 kg of fresh, non-sour curd in a muslin cloth over a strainer, put a weight on it and let the whey drain in the fridge for 4–5 hours or overnight. The thick hung curd left behind is chakka (roughly 380 g). Whisk it with powdered sugar to taste, a pinch of cardamom and saffron soaked in warm milk until smooth, then chill for 1–2 hours and top with nuts. Eat within about 2 days. Shrikhand (શ્રીખંડ, श्रीखंड) is a sweet, creamy dessert made from strained curd, and a Gujarati favourite with puri at festivals, weddings and Sunday lunches. People search for it as how to make shrikhand from dahi, shrikhand from hung curd, shrikhand kaise banaye ghar par, શ્રીખંડ બનાવવાની રીત or श्रीखंड कैसे बनाएं घर पर. This guide sums up what published home recipes and Indian dairy-technology material agree on, and shows which curd from our shop to use.

Note: this is a general home recipe. Shrikhand is not listed on our shop, and this article does not describe any Satvik process.

What is shrikhand, and what is chakka?

Dairy-technology texts describe shrikhand as a semi-solid, sweetish-sour fermented milk product. Dahi is drained of its whey to give chakka (also called maska), and sugar, flavour and spices are mixed into the chakka until it forms a soft, smooth mass. It is popular in Gujarat, Maharashtra and parts of Karnataka. A dairy review notes that people in Gujarat usually prefer a sweeter shrikhand, while Maharashtra prefers a tangier one.

For packaged shrikhand, the Indian standards quoted in dairy course material set at least 58% total solids, and sugar of at most 72.5% of the dry matter. At home, you simply sweeten to taste.

Shrikhand vs amrakhand

Amrakhand is shrikhand with mango pulp mixed in. Mango pulp, saffron, charoli (chironji), almonds, cashews, nutmeg and seasonal fruit are all listed as common additions in dairy texts.

You also need a muslin cloth (or a thin cotton cloth), a strainer, a bowl that catches the whey, and a weight such as a heavy bowl.

How to make shrikhand at home, step by step

Step 1: Start with fresh curd

Every recipe we checked stresses fresh, non-sour curd, because hanging concentrates the sourness. You can set it yourself from whole milk (see how to make curd at home) or use ready curd.

Step 2: Hang the curd to make chakka

  1. Line a strainer with muslin and set it over a deep bowl, so the strainer does not touch the whey.
  2. Pour in the curd, bring the corners of the cloth together and tie them.
  3. Press gently, then put a heavy bowl on top (or hang the bundle on a hook).
  4. Keep it in the fridge for 4–5 hours or overnight so it does not turn sour. Recipes range from 4 hours to 15–18 hours; dairy texts describe 10–12 hours of draining.
  5. It is ready when no more whey drips and the curd feels thick, like cream cheese.

Step 3: Sweeten and flavour

Put the chakka in a bowl, add the powdered sugar, saffron milk and cardamom, and whisk until the sugar dissolves and the mixture is completely smooth. An electric beater is quickest. Traditionally the chakka was passed through a sieve or a puran yantra for a silky texture. Taste and add more sugar if needed.

Step 4: Chill and serve

Cover and chill for 1–2 hours so it firms up and the flavours blend. Garnish with chopped pistachios, almonds or charoli. Serve with puri or on its own.

Short cut: recipes say thick Greek yogurt can replace hung curd, so you can skip Step 2.

How much shrikhand will 1 kg of curd give?

One published home recipe got about 425 g of chakka from about 1.125 kg of curd (made from 1 litre of whole milk). Dairy-technology sources say sugar is usually 50–100% of the chakka's weight and the yield is about 1.5–2 kg of shrikhand per kg of chakka. So 1 kg of curd gives very roughly 650–850 g of shrikhand, depending on how well it drains and how much sugar you add.

Do not throw away the whey. Recipes suggest using it to knead roti dough or in dal, curries and rice.

How to store homemade shrikhand

  • Keep it in a covered container in the fridge at 4 °C or below. Recipe guides suggest eating homemade shrikhand within about 2 days.
  • A dairy review notes shrikhand spoils within 2–3 days at about 30 °C. Longer shelf-life figures in that review are for factory-made product and do not apply to homemade shrikhand.
  • Do not leave it out for more than two hours (one hour in very hot weather), as the US FDA advises for perishable food.
  • Use a clean, dry spoon, and throw it away if it smells off or shows mould.

Common problems and simple fixes

Shrikhand is runny

The curd was not drained long enough. Hang it longer with a heavier weight next time. If it is already sweetened, chilling for a few hours firms it a little.

Shrikhand is grainy

The sugar has not dissolved. Use powdered sugar, rest it in the fridge for a few hours and whisk again.

Shrikhand is too sour

The curd was sour or drained at room temperature. Use fresh curd and always drain it in the fridge.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make shrikhand from curd at home?

Tie fresh, non-sour curd in a muslin cloth over a strainer and let the whey drain in the fridge for 4–5 hours or overnight, with a weight on top. The thick hung curd left behind is called chakka. Whisk it with powdered sugar, cardamom and saffron soaked in a little warm milk until smooth, then chill for 1–2 hours and garnish with nuts.

How long should I hang curd for shrikhand?

Published recipes hang the curd for about 4–5 hours to overnight in the fridge with a weight on top; one recipe leaves it 15–18 hours. Dairy-technology texts describe 10–12 hours of draining. Drain it in the fridge so the curd does not turn sour, and continue until no more whey drips.

How much shrikhand do I get from 1 kg of curd?

One published home recipe got about 425 g of hung curd (chakka) from about 1.125 kg of curd made from 1 litre of whole milk. Dairy-technology sources give about 1.5–2 kg of shrikhand per kg of chakka, depending on how much sugar is added, so 1 kg of curd gives very roughly 650–850 g of shrikhand.

How much sugar goes into shrikhand?

Dairy-technology sources say sugar is usually 50–100% of the weight of the chakka. Home recipes use less and add it to taste, for example 8–9 tablespoons of sugar for the chakka from about 1.125 kg of curd. Powdered sugar dissolves faster and gives a smoother texture.

Can I use full-fat or non-fat curd for shrikhand?

Both work. Recipes recommend curd made from whole milk for a thick, creamy shrikhand. Non-fat curd gives a lower-fat shrikhand; commercial plants often start from skim-milk curd and blend in cream later to reach the fat standard. Whichever you use, it should be fresh and not sour.

How long does homemade shrikhand last?

Recipe guides suggest keeping homemade shrikhand covered in the fridge and eating it within about 2 days. A dairy review notes that shrikhand spoils within 2–3 days at about 30 °C, so do not leave it out. Keep it at 4 °C or below and throw it away if it smells off or shows mould.
