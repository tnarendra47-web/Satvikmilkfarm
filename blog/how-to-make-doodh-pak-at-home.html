How to Make Doodh Pak at Home: Gujarati Recipe with Farm Milk (દૂધપાક)

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 27 November 2026

Quick answer: Boil 1 litre of full-fat milk in a heavy pan with a little ghee and let it reduce on low heat for a few minutes, stirring. Add just 1–2 tablespoons of washed, soaked rice and cook 10–15 minutes until soft, scraping the pan. Stir in about ¼ cup sugar, cook 5 minutes, then add cardamom, nutmeg, charoli, almonds and pistachios. Serve hot or chilled with puri. Doodh pak (દૂધપાક, दूध पाक) is the Gujarati milk-and-rice sweet served with hot puri at festivals, family gatherings and during Shraddh. People search for it as doodh pak recipe gujarati, દૂધપાક બનાવવાની રીત, dudh pak kaise banate hain or doodh pak vs kheer. This guide sums up what published Gujarati recipes agree on, and where they differ.

Note: this is a general home recipe. Doodh pak is not sold on our shop, and this article does not describe any Satvik process. We sell the milk and ghee you can make it with.

What is doodh pak?

Doodh pak means "cooked milk" in Gujarati. Full-fat milk is simmered until it reduces and turns creamy, a small amount of rice is cooked in it, and it is sweetened and flavoured with cardamom, nutmeg and nuts such as charoli (chironji), almonds and pistachios. It is thinner than rabri or basundi and is meant to be creamy but almost drinkable.

How to make doodh pak, step by step

Step 1: Soak the rice

Wash 1–2 tablespoons of rice two or three times and soak it in water for 10–15 minutes. Drain it well and mix in a little ghee so the grains stay separate.

Step 2: Boil the milk in a heavy pan

Grease a heavy-bottomed pan with ghee, pour in the milk and bring it to a boil on medium heat, stirring so it does not stick or boil over.

Step 3: Let the milk reduce

Lower the heat and simmer for several minutes, stirring and scraping the sides, until the milk turns slightly thick and creamy. If you use saffron, add it now.

Step 4: Cook the rice in the milk

Add the drained rice and cook on low heat for 10–15 minutes, stirring often, until a grain presses easily between your fingers.

Step 5: Add sugar

Add the sugar and cook about 5 minutes more until it dissolves.

Step 6: Flavour and finish

Stir in cardamom, nuts and finally nutmeg, boil for a minute or two and switch off while it is still a little thinner than you want. It thickens as it cools.

Which milk to use for doodh pak

Every recipe we checked asks for full-fat milk. Tarla Dalal's recipe uses whole buffalo milk, which gives a richer doodh pak. One Gujarati recipe notes that many families use cow milk when making doodh pak for Shraddh and buffalo milk at other times. Skimmed milk will make a thin, less creamy doodh pak. For the difference between the two milks, read cow milk vs buffalo milk.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Too much rice: keep to 1–2 tablespoons per litre; more rice turns it into kheer.
  • Milk sticking or burning: use a heavy pan, keep the heat low and scrape the sides and bottom.
  • Too thick after cooling: switch off early; stir in a little warm milk if needed.
  • Saffron or no saffron: recipes differ. Some add saffron; others say traditional Gujarati doodh pak stays white, flavoured only with cardamom, nutmeg and nuts.
  • Sweetener: one Gujarati recipe advises avoiding jaggery with milk here and suggests sugar instead.

How to serve and store doodh pak

  • Serve hot or chilled, traditionally with puri.
  • Cool it, cover it and keep it in the fridge at 4 °C or below.
  • Do not leave it out for more than two hours (one hour in very hot weather), as the US FDA advises for perishable food.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make doodh pak at home?

Wash and soak 1–2 tablespoons of rice for 10–15 minutes. Boil 1 litre of full-fat milk in a heavy pan with a little ghee, lower the heat and let it reduce for a few minutes while stirring. Add the drained rice and cook 10–15 minutes until soft, stirring and scraping the pan. Add about ¼ cup sugar and cook 5 minutes, then add cardamom, nutmeg, charoli, almonds and pistachios. Serve hot or chilled with puri.

What is the difference between doodh pak and kheer?

Both are milk-and-rice sweets. Doodh pak uses very little rice (about 1–3 tablespoons per litre of milk in published Gujarati recipes) and stays thin enough to almost drink. Kheer usually uses much more rice (around ¼ cup per litre) and is thicker. Doodh pak means "cooked milk" in Gujarati: the milk is reduced first and the rice is added after.

How much rice do I need for 1 litre of milk?

Gujarati recipes use about 1 to 2 tablespoons of rice for 1 litre of milk; some go up to 3 tablespoons. Recipe writers warn that if you add more rice, you end up with kheer instead of doodh pak. Long-grain rice such as basmati is commonly used.

Which milk is best for doodh pak, cow or buffalo?

Full-fat milk is recommended in every recipe we checked because it thickens better. Tarla Dalal's recipe uses whole buffalo milk (6.5% fat). One Gujarati recipe notes that families making doodh pak for Shraddh use cow milk, and buffalo milk otherwise. Either works; buffalo milk gives a richer result.

Should I add saffron to doodh pak?

Recipes differ. Many add a few saffron strands soaked in warm milk. Others say traditional Gujarati doodh pak stays white and is flavoured only with cardamom, nutmeg and nuts. It is a matter of family taste.

Why did my doodh pak become too thick?

Doodh pak keeps thickening as it cools, so switch off the stove while it is still a little thinner than you want. Too much rice also makes it thick like kheer. If it is too thick, stir in a little warm milk.

How long can I keep doodh pak?

Cool it, cover it and keep it in the fridge at 4 °C or below; many families serve it chilled. Do not leave it out at room temperature for more than two hours (one hour in very hot weather), as the US FDA advises for perishable food.
