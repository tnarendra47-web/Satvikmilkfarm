How to Make Thick Malai at Home from Milk: Step by Step

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 22 November 2026

Quick answer: Boil 1 litre of full-fat or buffalo milk in a wide, heavy pan, then let it simmer for about 5 minutes. Switch off the heat and leave it uncovered and undisturbed until it reaches room temperature (about 2–4 hours). Refrigerate overnight, then loosen the edges and lift off the thick layer of malai with a flat spatula. The higher the fat in the milk, the thicker the malai. Malai (મલાઈ, मलाई) is the thick layer that sets on top of milk after you boil and cool it. Families use it in curries and sweets, spread it on paratha, and collect it to make butter and ghee. People search for it as how to make malai at home with milk, malai kaise banaye, dudh malai kaise banaye, मलाई कैसे बनाये or ઘરે મલાઈ કેવી રીતે બનાવવી. This guide sums up what published home recipes and dairy-science sources agree on, and shows which milk from our shop to use.

What is malai, and how is it different from cream?

The FAO's guide to traditional milk products describes malai as the firm skin that forms on cooling boiled milk: a mixture of milk fat and coagulated (heat-set) milk proteins, white to yellowish, with a fat content of about 20–30%. Cream is different: it is the fatty part that rises to the top of milk that has not been boiled, or that is separated by machine.

A study in the Indian Journal of Dairy Science (NDRI, Bengaluru) describes good malai as yellowish, with a rich nutty aroma and a soft, flaky texture.

How to make thick malai at home, step by step

Step 1: Boil the milk in a wide pan

Pour the milk into a wide, heavy-bottomed pan and bring it to a boil on medium to high heat. Keep a spatula across the pan or stay close so it does not boil over.

Step 2: Simmer for about 5 minutes

Lower the heat and let the milk simmer gently for about 5 minutes (recipes range from 2 to 10 minutes). Simmering drives off some water and helps a firmer skin form. Do not stir hard.

Step 3: Cool it uncovered and undisturbed

Switch off the heat and leave the pan uncovered on the counter until it reaches room temperature, about 2–4 hours. Do not stir or move it. A lid traps steam, which drips back onto the surface and gives thinner, watery malai.

Step 4: Chill overnight

Once cool, place the pan in the fridge for 8–12 hours or overnight. The cold firms the fat so the malai sets into a thick sheet.

Step 5: Lift off the malai

Run a knife around the edge of the pan to loosen the layer, then lift it off with a flat spatula or slotted spoon and put it in your container. The milk underneath is still good to drink or to use for curd or paneer. If you boil it again, it will form a second, thinner layer.

Tips for thicker malai

  • Use higher-fat milk. Buffalo milk can have up to about twice the fat of cow milk (a 2022 review of buffalo milk research), which is why it gives the thickest malai. Full-fat cow milk also works well.
  • Use a wide pan. In the Indian Journal of Dairy Science study, malai yield went up as the exposed surface area of the milk increased.
  • Heat for longer, gently. Under controlled conditions that study got the most malai by holding milk at 90 °C for 50 minutes, then letting malai form as it cooled to 30 °C. At home, a gentle simmer of a few minutes followed by slow cooling works on the same idea.
  • Homogenised milk gives thinner layers. Homogenisation breaks up the fat globules so less fat rises to the surface; a popular rabri recipe notes it gives thinner malai layers.
  • Collect every day. Many home cooks collect the malai each time they boil milk and keep it in one container in the fridge.

How to store homemade malai

  • Keep it in a clean, airtight container in the fridge at 4 °C or below, and use a clean, dry spoon each time.
  • Many home cooks keep adding to the same container for 10–15 days before making butter or ghee. Throw it away if it smells sour or rancid or shows mould.
  • The 18-day shelf life at 5 °C reported in the dairy study was for malai made and packed under controlled conditions; it is not a guide for homemade malai.
  • Do not leave malai out for more than two hours (one hour in very hot weather), as the US FDA advises for perishable food.

What to make with malai

  • Ghee: collect malai for several days, then cook it down. See our guide to making ghee at home from malai.
  • Butter (makhan): whisk or blend chilled malai with cold water until white butter separates.
  • Sweets: malai is the base of rabri and can be stirred into kheer or kulfi.
  • Cooking: use it like fresh cream in curries, or spread it on paratha or bread.
  • Drinks: blend a spoonful into lassi. Read our lassi and malai lassi guide.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make thick malai at home from milk?

Pour 1 litre of full-fat or buffalo milk into a wide, heavy pan and bring it to a boil. Lower the heat and let it simmer for about 5 minutes without stirring hard. Switch off the heat and leave the pan uncovered and undisturbed until it reaches room temperature (about 2–4 hours), then refrigerate it overnight. Next morning loosen the edges with a knife and lift the thick layer of malai off with a flat spatula or slotted spoon.

Which milk gives the thickest malai?

Higher-fat milk gives thicker malai. Recipes recommend full-fat (full cream) milk and say buffalo milk gives the thickest layer because it has more fat. Skimmed or low-fat milk gives only a thin skin, and homogenised milk tends to give thinner malai layers.

What is malai?

Malai is the layer that forms on top of milk after it is boiled and cooled. The FAO describes it as a firm skin made of milk fat and coagulated (heat-set) milk proteins, white to yellowish in colour. That is why it is different from cream, which simply rises to the top of unboiled milk.

Why is the malai on my milk thin?

The usual reasons are low-fat or homogenised milk, a narrow pan, not simmering long enough, covering the pan while it cools (steam drips back on the surface), or skimming before the milk is fully chilled. A wider pan exposes more surface, and a dairy study found malai yield went up as the exposed surface area increased.

How long can I store homemade malai?

Keep it in a clean, airtight container in the fridge at 4 °C or below and use a clean, dry spoon. Many home cooks keep adding malai to the same container in the fridge for 10–15 days to make butter or ghee. Throw it away if it smells sour or rancid or shows mould. Do not leave it at room temperature for more than two hours.

What can I make with homemade malai?

You can use it like fresh cream in curries, spread it on bread or paratha, add it to lassi or kheer, whisk it into homemade butter (makhan), or collect it for a few days and cook it into ghee.
