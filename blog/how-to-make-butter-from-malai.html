How to Make Butter (Makhan) from Malai at Home: Mixer and Hand Method

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 23 November 2026

Quick answer: Collect malai in the fridge for a few days. Put the chilled malai in a mixer jar with a few ice cubes and about half a cup of ice-cold water, blend in short bursts, add more cold water and blend again until white butter floats and thin chaas separates. Lift out the butter, wash it in ice-cold water until the water stays clear, shape it and refrigerate. Without a mixer, churn the same way with a whisk or wooden mathani. White butter, makhan (માખણ, मक्खन), is what you get when you churn malai until the fat comes together and the liquid separates. Many families in Gujarat and across India still make it at home from the malai they collect each day. People search for it as how to make butter from malai at home, how to make butter from malai in mixer, dudh ki malai se makhan kaise banaye, मलाई से मक्खन कैसे बनाएं or મલાઈમાંથી માખણ કેવી રીતે બનાવવું. This guide sums up what published home recipes and dairy-science sources agree on.

Note: this is a general home method. Butter is not listed on our shop, and this article does not describe any Satvik process. To make malai first, see our guide to thick malai at home.

What is makhan (white butter)?

Makhan is traditional Indian butter made by churning dahi or malai. A 2020 review of makhan production in the Journal of Food Science and Technology gives its average composition as about 78.5–81.3% fat and 18–20% moisture, and notes that FSSAI requires makhan to contain at least 76% milk fat. Compared with factory butter it has more moisture, curd particles and lactic acid, which gives it its fresh flavour but also means it keeps for a shorter time.

The same review describes the old household practice: malai is collected for several days, often soured with a little dahi, and churned early in the morning in an earthen pot with a wooden beater, adding cold or warm water depending on the season.

How to make butter from malai in a mixer, step by step

Step 1: Collect and chill the malai

Each time you boil milk, lift off the malai and add it to one clean container in the fridge. Recipes collect it for about a week; one uses the malai from 8 litres of milk. Keep it cold until you churn.

Step 2 (optional): Culture it with curd

For a desi, slightly tangy makhan, bring the malai to room temperature, stir in about 2 tablespoons of curd and leave it for about 6 hours, then chill it again before churning. You can skip this step and churn plain chilled malai.

Step 3: Blend with ice and cold water

Put the malai in the mixer jar (do not fill it more than about half). Add 10–12 ice cubes and about half a cup of ice-cold water. Blend for about 30 seconds in short pulses. Add about 1 cup more cold water and blend for another 30 seconds or so.

Step 4: Wait for the butter to separate

Open the jar. When it is ready you will see pale lumps of white butter floating on a thin, watery liquid. If it still looks like thick whipped cream, blend a little longer, a few seconds at a time.

Step 5: Collect, press and wash the butter

Scoop the butter into a bowl of ice-cold water. Press and fold it with a spoon or your hands to squeeze out the trapped liquid, change the water and repeat until the water stays almost clear. Washing out the buttermilk well helps the butter keep longer.

Step 6: Shape and set

Shape the butter into a ball or block (butter paper helps), put it in a clean airtight container and refrigerate it for about 30 minutes to set.

How to make butter from malai without a mixer

Put the malai in a deep steel bowl or earthen pot and add ice-cold water. Churn with a wooden mathani (ravai), spinning it back and forth between your palms or with a rope, or use a balloon whisk. It takes longer than a mixer, often several minutes of steady churning, but you will see the same signs: the malai thickens, then grains of butter form and float. Collect and wash them as in Step 5. This is the traditional way described in dairy textbooks.

Making butter from malai in winter and summer

Temperature decides how quickly butter forms. Dairy-science teaching material from the University of Guelph gives the best cream churning temperature as about 7–10 °C in summer and 10–13 °C in winter. If the cream is too warm the butter turns soft and greasy and more fat is lost in the buttermilk; if it is too cold the fat is hard and churning slows down.

In summer

  • Use malai straight from the fridge, plenty of ice and ice-cold water.
  • Chill the mixer jar and the washing water too, and work quickly.
  • If the butter turns soft, put the jar in the fridge for 15–20 minutes and churn again.

In winter

  • Very cold malai can be hard and slow to churn. Let it stand out for a short while so it softens a little.
  • Use cool rather than ice-cold water. The makhan review notes that households add cold or warm water depending on the season.

How much butter will I get?

There is no fixed yield, because malai holds different amounts of water and milk solids. Published home recipes give a rough idea:

  • About 600 g of butter from 1 kg of malai collected over 8–10 days from toned milk (Healthically Kitchen), with more from full-fat milk.
  • About 250 g of butter from a week's malai of 8 litres of milk (Aayansh Kitchen).

A CBSE dairy technology textbook notes that the desi method recovers only about 75–85% of the fat, so some fat always stays in the chaas.

Common problems and simple fixes

The malai turns into whipped cream but not butter

Keep blending in short bursts. Cream thickens first and then suddenly breaks into butter and liquid. Add a few more ice cubes if it feels warm.

The butter is soft, greasy or melting

It is too warm. Chill the jar and the butter, then wash it in fresh ice water.

The butter smells sour or goes bad quickly

Buttermilk is still trapped inside or the malai was stored too long. Wash the butter until the water runs clear, and use malai that has been kept cold in a clean container.

How to store homemade butter

  • Keep it in a clean, airtight container in the fridge at 4 °C or below, and use a clean, dry spoon.
  • Home recipe sources suggest using it within about 1–2 weeks. Throw it away if it smells sour or rancid or shows mould.
  • Do not leave it out for more than two hours (one hour in very hot weather), as the US FDA advises for perishable food.
  • To keep it longer, cook it into ghee. See our guide to making ghee at home from malai and makhan.

What to do with the leftover chaas

The thin liquid left after churning is buttermilk (chaas). Drink it chilled, add salt and jeera for masala chaas, or use it in kadhi or to knead dough. It is thinner than shop chaas when you have used a lot of ice water. Read our chaas vs masala chaas guide.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make butter from malai at home?

Collect malai in the fridge for a few days. Put the chilled malai in a mixer jar with a few ice cubes and about half a cup of ice-cold water and blend in short bursts. Add more cold water and blend again until lumps of white butter float on top and a thin liquid (chaas) separates. Lift out the butter, press out the liquid, wash it in a bowl of ice-cold water until the water stays clear, then shape it and refrigerate it.

How do I make butter from malai without a mixer?

Use a hand whisk or a wooden churner (mathani/ravai). Put the malai in a deep bowl or pot, add ice-cold water and churn steadily in one direction or back and forth. It takes longer than a mixer, but the same thing happens: grains of butter form and float on the liquid. Collect, press and wash them in cold water.

Why is my malai not turning into butter?

Temperature is the usual reason. Dairy-science sources say that if cream is too warm the butter turns soft and greasy and more fat is lost in the buttermilk, and if it is too cold the fat is hard and churning takes much longer. In summer add more ice and chill the jar; in winter let very cold malai soften a little or use cool rather than ice-cold water. Too little malai in a big jar also makes churning slow.

How much butter do I get from malai?

It depends on the milk and on how much water the malai holds. One home cook reports about 600 g of butter from 1 kg of malai collected over 8–10 days from toned milk, and another gets about 250 g from a week's malai of 8 litres of milk. Malai from higher-fat milk such as buffalo milk usually gives more.

Should I add curd to malai before making butter?

It is optional. The traditional desi way is to set the malai or milk with a little dahi first and churn the cultured malai, which gives makhan a slightly tangy flavour. Some recipes mix about 2 tablespoons of curd into the malai at room temperature and rest it for about 6 hours before churning. Plain chilled malai also works.

How long does homemade white butter last?

Keep it in a clean airtight container in the fridge at 4 °C or below. Home recipe sources suggest using it within about 1–2 weeks. Desi makhan holds more moisture, curd and lactic acid than shop butter, so it keeps less well; washing out the buttermilk well helps. Throw it away if it smells sour or rancid or shows mould.

What do I do with the liquid left after making butter?

The thin liquid left after churning is buttermilk (chaas). You can drink it, spice it as masala chaas, or use it in kadhi or dough. If you used ice water it will be thinner than shop chaas.
