How to Make Curd (Dahi) at Home with Farm Milk: Step by Step

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 16 November 2026

Quick answer: Bring the milk to a boil, cool it until it is lukewarm (about 40–45 °C), stir in about 1 teaspoon to 1 tablespoon of fresh curd per litre, cover and leave it undisturbed in a warm place for about 4–7 hours in warm weather or 8–12 hours in cold weather, then refrigerate. Which milk to use for thick curd

Recipe guides consistently say that full-fat milk sets thicker, creamier curd, while toned or skimmed milk sets thinner curd. Buffalo milk generally has more fat than cow milk, which is why many people use it for thick dahi; see our cow milk vs buffalo milk guide and full-fat vs skimmed milk guide.

Step-by-step: how to set curd at home

  1. Boil the milk. Use a heavy, clean pan and stir now and then so it does not stick. Recipe guides often simmer it a few minutes longer for thicker curd.
  2. Cool it to lukewarm. About 40–45 °C. Published recipe guides give ranges of roughly 39–46 °C. Without a thermometer, dip a clean finger: it should feel warm, not hot.
  3. Add the starter (jaman / મેળવણ). About 1 teaspoon of fresh plain curd per litre in hot weather and up to 1 tablespoon in cold weather. Whisk it with a spoon of the milk first, then mix it in well.
  4. Cover and leave it alone. Pour into a clean steel, glass or clay bowl, cover, and keep it in a warm spot. Do not move or stir it while it sets.
  5. Check after a few hours. It usually takes about 4–7 hours in warm weather and 8–12 hours in cold weather. Tilt the bowl gently: set curd holds its shape.
  6. Refrigerate. Move it to the fridge as soon as it has set; this slows further souring.

Common problems and simple fixes

Curd did not set or is very loose

The milk was probably too cold when the starter went in, the room was cold, or the starter was old. In winter, recipe guides suggest slightly warmer milk, a little more starter, and keeping the bowl in a closed oven, casserole or wrapped in a cloth.

Curd is watery

Starter added to milk that was too hot, or the bowl was moved while setting, can make curd release water (whey). Let the milk cool to lukewarm first and keep the bowl still.

Curd is too sour

A very sour starter, too much starter, or leaving it out too long after it has set all add sourness. Use fresh curd as starter and refrigerate on time.

How warm should the milk be when I add the starter?

Lukewarm, roughly 40–45 °C. Published recipe guides give ranges of about 39–46 °C. Without a thermometer, dip a clean finger: the milk should feel warm but not hot. Very hot milk can make the curd watery; cold milk sets slowly.

How much starter (jaman) do I need for 1 litre of milk?

Recipe guides suggest about 1 teaspoon to 1 tablespoon of fresh plain curd per litre: less in hot weather, more in cold weather. Use fresh curd as starter; a very sour starter tends to give sour curd.

How long does curd take to set?

Usually about 4–7 hours in warm weather and 8–12 hours in cold weather. Keep the covered bowl in a warm place without moving it, then refrigerate as soon as it has set.
