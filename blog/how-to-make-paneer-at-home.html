How to Make Paneer at Home from Farm Milk: Step by Step

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 17 November 2026

Quick answer: Heat 1 litre of full-fat milk to about 85–90 °C (steaming, just about to rise), lower the heat and stir in about 1–1½ tablespoons of lemon juice or vinegar diluted in a little water, a little at a time, until white curds separate from pale greenish whey. Strain through a muslin cloth, rinse briefly and press for about 30–40 minutes for cubes. Guides put the yield at about 150–200 g of paneer per litre. On 17 November 2026 our shop page lists Full Fat Milk at ₹70 per litre and Malai Paneer [250g] at ₹150.

Fresh paneer (cottage cheese, chhena) is easy to make at home with just milk and an acid such as lemon juice. Which milk to use for paneer

Recipe guides recommend full-fat (whole) milk. Richer milk gives more paneer and a softer texture; leaner milk gives less, firmer paneer. Buffalo milk usually has more fat and solids than cow milk, so it generally yields more paneer per litre. See our cow milk vs buffalo milk guide and full-fat vs skimmed milk guide.

Step-by-step: how to make paneer at home

What you need (for 1 litre of milk)

  • 1 litre full-fat milk
  • 1–1½ tablespoons (15–22 ml) lemon juice or plain white vinegar, mixed with 2–3 tablespoons of water
  • A heavy-bottomed pan, a strainer and a clean muslin cloth (or thin cotton cloth)

Method

  1. Heat the milk. Warm it over medium heat, stirring so the bottom does not scorch, until it is steaming and just about to rise (about 85–90 °C). A violent rolling boil is not needed.
  2. Lower the heat and add the acid slowly. Add the diluted lemon juice or vinegar about a tablespoon at a time, stirring gently.
  3. Stop when it splits. You want soft white curds and clear, pale yellow-green whey. If the liquid still looks milky after a minute or two, add a little more acid.
  4. Let it rest. Leave the curds in the hot whey for 5–10 minutes without much stirring so they gather.
  5. Strain and rinse. Pour through the cloth-lined strainer and rinse briefly with cool water to remove the lemony taste.
  6. Press. Twist the cloth, place the bundle under a heavy pot: about 10 minutes for soft crumbled paneer (bhurji), 30–40 minutes for cubes, longer for firmer tikka-style paneer.
  7. Cut and store. Cut into cubes and keep covered (or in fresh cold water) in the fridge; guides suggest using it within 2–3 days.

How much paneer from 1 litre of milk?

Published guides give about 150–200 g of paneer from 1 litre of full-fat milk. One paneer-maker's guide gives about 150–170 g from cow milk and 200–225 g from buffalo milk, and another guide about 150 g from low-fat milk. The real figure depends on the milk and on how hard you press.

Common problems and simple fixes

Paneer is hard or rubbery

Usually too much acid, too much heat after splitting, or pressing too long. Lower the heat before adding acid, add it slowly and stop as soon as the whey turns clear.

Paneer crumbles when cut

It was not pressed long enough, or it was made from lean milk. Press it longer and chill it before cutting.

Milk is not splitting

The milk may not be hot enough or needs a little more acid. Bring it back up to steaming and add a teaspoon more of diluted acid at a time.
