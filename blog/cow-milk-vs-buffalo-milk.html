Cow Milk vs Buffalo Milk: Which Is Better for Your Family?

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Prices checked 5 November 2026

Quick answer: Neither milk is "better" for everyone. Buffalo milk is thicker and richer: it generally has more fat and calories (about 6.6 g fat and 107 kcal per 100 g, vs 4.5 g and 73 kcal for cow milk, per ICMR-NIN's Indian Food Composition Tables 2017), so it suits malai, dahi, paneer and sweets. Cow milk is lighter for everyday drinking and chai. Protein and calcium are similar. At Satvik Milk Farm both are ₹80 per litre, so choose by taste and use. For children, ask your paediatrician.

"Gaay ka doodh ya bhains ka doodh?" (गाय का दूध या भैंस का दूध? / ગાયનું દૂધ કે ભેંસનું દૂધ?) is one of the most common questions families in Vapi and Valsad ask. We keep both Gir cows and buffaloes on our farm in Borlai, so here is a plain, sourced comparison to help you decide.

Cow milk vs buffalo milk: nutrition per 100 g

The main difference is fat and calories; protein and calcium are close. These are average values for whole milk from India's official food composition tables, not figures for any one farm's milk.

Per 100 g (whole milk)Cow milkBuffalo milk
Energy~73 kcal~107 kcal
Fat~4.5 g~6.6 g
Protein~3.3 g~3.7 g
Calcium~118 mg~121 mg

Source: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017 (IFCT 2017), ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad (entries "Milk, whole, Cow" and "Milk, whole, Buffalo"). Values are regional averages; actual milk varies with breed, feed and season.

A 200 ml glass works out to roughly 146 kcal for cow milk and 215 kcal for buffalo milk (simple arithmetic from the table above).

What do FSSAI standards say?

FSSAI requires milk sold as buffalo milk to have more fat and solids than milk sold as cow milk. Under the Food Safety and Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations, 2011 (sub-regulation 2.1.2, Milk), the all-India minimums are:

Class of milkMinimum milk fatMinimum SNF (solids-not-fat)
Buffalo milk5.0%9.0%
Cow milk3.2%8.3%
Skimmed milknot more than 0.5%8.7%

Source: FSSAI Compendium, Chapter 2.1 Dairy products and analogues. These are legal minimums at the point of sale, not average values.

The UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) also notes that buffalo milk's fat content is on average about twice that of cow milk, and that its higher casein content helps in cheese (paneer) making.

Taste, texture and kitchen use

Pick buffalo milk for thickness, cow milk for a lighter glass.

  • Colour and texture: buffalo milk is whiter and creamier; cow milk is lighter and slightly yellowish.
  • Malai, dahi, paneer, sweets: buffalo milk's extra fat and solids give thicker malai, firmer dahi and more paneer per litre, which is why many Gujarati households use it for mithai and ghee.
  • Everyday drinking and chai: many families prefer cow milk because it feels lighter.
  • Calories: if you are watching fat or calories, cow milk (or skimmed milk) has less per glass.

Cow milk vs buffalo milk for kids

Ask your paediatrician; there is no one answer for every child. Milk needs change with age, weight and health, and babies under one year have their own feeding guidance. Your doctor can tell you which milk, how much, and whether to dilute it. We don't make health claims for our milk.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, cow milk or buffalo milk?

Neither is better for everyone. Buffalo milk generally has more fat and calories (about 6.6 g fat and 107 kcal per 100 g vs 4.5 g and 73 kcal for cow milk, ICMR-NIN IFCT 2017), so it is thicker and suits malai, dahi and paneer. Cow milk is lighter for everyday drinking. Choose by taste, use and your family's needs.

Is cow milk or buffalo milk better for kids?

It depends on the child's age and needs, so please ask your paediatrician before choosing or switching milk, especially for babies under one year and toddlers. Buffalo milk generally has more fat and calories than cow milk.
