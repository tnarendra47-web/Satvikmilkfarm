How to Make Ghee at Home from Malai: Step by Step

By Satvik Milk Farm, Kumbhariya, Borlai (Pardi taluka, Valsad) · Updated 18 November 2026

Quick answer: Collect the malai from boiled full-fat milk in a covered box in the fridge (4 °C or below) or freezer. Stir in a spoonful of curd and let it set, then churn it with cold water until makhan (butter) separates. Wash the butter, melt it in a heavy pan on a low flame, and stir until the bubbling turns fine, the solids at the bottom are golden to light brown and it smells nutty. Strain through muslin into a dry glass jar. You can also heat the malai directly, which is quicker but leaves more residue. Two ways to make ghee from malai

Method 1: heat the malai directly

Melt the collected malai in a heavy-bottomed pan and keep stirring while the water boils off, then lower the flame and stop when the solids turn golden. It needs no setting or churning, but the malai still holds a lot of water and milk solids, so it takes longer in the pan and leaves more brown residue.

Method 2: malai → dahi-set → makhan → ghee (the desi way)

Set the malai with a little curd, churn it into butter and then clarify the butter. It adds a day of setting and a few minutes of churning, but much of the water and non-fat solids leave in the buttermilk (chaas), so the pan stage is shorter. Recipe guides describe the result as nuttier with less residue. The steps below follow this method.

Step 1: collect and store malai safely

  • Use full-fat or buffalo milk: boil it, let it cool undisturbed, then lift the thick layer of malai with a clean, dry spoon.
  • Keep the malai in a covered container in the fridge at 4 °C or below, or in the freezer if you collect over many days. Thaw frozen malai in the fridge, not on the counter.
  • Do not leave milk or malai at room temperature for more than two hours (one hour when it is above about 32 °C). This is the US FDA's general rule for perishable food.
  • Throw away malai that smells sour, cheesy or mouldy. Heating spoiled cream into ghee does not make it good again.

Step-by-step: how to make ghee at home from malai

What you need

  • Collected malai (cold), 1–2 tablespoons of fresh curd (dahi), cold or iced water
  • A mixer jar or hand churner (valonu / madhani), a heavy-bottomed kadai or pan
  • A steel strainer or clean, dry muslin cloth and a completely dry glass or steel jar

Method

  1. Set the malai: bring it to room temperature, mix in the curd, cover and leave it until it sets and smells clean and lightly sour (usually overnight). Chill it again before churning.
  2. Churn: blend or churn in short bursts, adding cold water a little at a time, until butter clumps float apart from thin buttermilk.
  3. Wash the butter: lift the makhan into a bowl of cold water, press and rinse it 2–3 times until the water runs clearer, then drain. Keep the buttermilk for chaas or cooking.
  4. Melt slowly: put the butter in the heavy pan on a low to medium-low flame, uncovered. It will foam, splutter and crackle as the water boils off. Stir and scrape the base now and then.
  5. Watch the finish: when the loud crackling fades, a finer foam appears and the solids at the bottom turn golden, turn off the flame. The fat heats quickly once the water has gone.
  6. Strain and cool: let the solids settle for a minute, then pour the ghee through the strainer or muslin into the dry jar. Leave the lid loose until it cools, then close it.

How to tell when ghee is ready

Look for four signs together: the noisy frothing has calmed, a second round of small bubbles has formed, the residue at the bottom is golden to light brown, and the kitchen smells nutty. In dairies, the direct-cream method heats cream to about 110–120 °C before the residue is filtered off. Dark brown residue and a burnt or fishy smell mean the ghee was overheated.

How much ghee will I get?

There is no fixed yield from a bowl of homemade malai. It depends on the fat in the milk, how much milk stays mixed in the malai, and how well the butter is churned, washed and drained. Weigh the malai and the finished ghee from your first batch so you know what to expect next time.

How to store homemade ghee

  • Use a clean, completely dry jar with a tight lid, and always take ghee out with a dry spoon. Water and food bits shorten its life.
  • A dairy-technology textbook gives a normal shelf life of 6–8 months at room temperature for ghee, with oxidised (rancid) flavour as the usual limit. Keep it away from heat and light.
  • Homemade ghee may set smooth, grainy or both. Texture alone does not prove purity.

Common problems and simple fixes

Ghee smells burnt or the residue is dark

The flame was too high at the end. Next time lower the heat as soon as the crackling fades and take the pan off when the solids are golden.

Too much splutter and foam

The butter still held a lot of water or buttermilk. Wash and drain it better, and keep the pan uncovered so steam can escape.

Butter will not separate when churning

The malai is usually too warm. Chill it, add more ice-cold water and churn again in short bursts.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make ghee directly from malai without making butter?

Yes. You can heat the collected malai slowly in a heavy pan until the water boils off and the milk solids turn golden, then strain it. Recipe guides say this direct method is quicker but leaves more brown residue, while setting the malai with curd and churning it into butter (makhan) first removes much of the water and solids in the buttermilk.

How long can I collect malai before making ghee?

There is no single safe number of days. Keep the malai covered in a fridge at 4 °C or below, or in the freezer for longer collection, and do not leave it out for more than two hours (one hour in very hot weather), as the US FDA advises for perishable food. Throw away any malai that smells sour, cheesy or mouldy.

How do I know the ghee is ready?

The loud frothing and crackling dies down, a second round of finer bubbles appears, the solids at the bottom turn golden to light brown and the pan smells nutty. Take it off the heat then. Dark brown solids and a burnt smell mean it has been overheated. In dairies, the direct-cream method heats to about 110–120 °C.

How much ghee do I get from malai?

There is no fixed yield. It depends on how much fat the milk had, how much milk stays in the malai, and how well the butter is churned and drained. Weigh the malai and the finished ghee from your first batch to learn your own yield.

How long does homemade ghee last?

A dairy-technology textbook gives a normal shelf life of 6–8 months at room temperature for ghee, with oxidised flavour as the usual limit. At home, keep it in a clean, completely dry jar, use a dry spoon and discard it if it smells rancid.
